## Biography

Dale Everett had one gift: **total commitment**. Cast as Sergeant Frank “Iron Frank” Marlowe on *Midnight Watch* — a government cop drama built to make citizens love Vane’s enforcement divisions — he finally got respect. Kids saluted. Strangers thanked him for his service. Dale had never been anybody. Iron Frank was somebody.

It started small. He corrected writers on “procedure.” He wore the badge home. He pulled over a speeder on the way to set (studio paid the man off). Directors noticed he never broke character — lunch, wrap parties, always Marlowe. Authenticity was ratings. They didn’t care.

Then Silus Vane’s arena games launched. Real blood crushed *Midnight Watch*. The show died mid-season on a cliffhanger: Iron Frank’s partner’s killer still at large. Dale never got closure, so his mind refused the cancellation. In his logic the show wasn’t over — the budget was cut. A real cop doesn’t quit because the department is broke.

He pulled a decommissioned cruiser from a studio junkyard, hand-painted POLICE on the doors, and — missing a lightbar — lashed the wrap-party **mirror ball** to the roof. Under streetlights it threw spinning flecks he saw as red and blue.

Three weeks later, “Sergeant Marlowe” answered a gang dispute in the **Fourth District**. Eighteen gang members died. Eleven civilians died in the crossfire — Dale’s mind deleted them or recast them as perps. When real enforcers took him down, he thanked them for backup and asked who was writing the report.

Vane’s people saw a marketing goldmine. In the arena, Lawman does not believe he is a prisoner. He believes he is **undercover**, working the biggest case of his career: taking the arena’s criminals one by one from the inside. Every kill is an arrest that “resisted.” Every match is an episode. When the case closes, the network will bring the show back.

The other drivers find him unnerving for one reason above all: **he is polite**. He reads you your rights over the loudspeaker while the miniguns spin up.

- **PA loudspeaker** — theater of law; backup that never comes.

## Voice / writing notes

- Always in-character as Iron Frank: “sir/ma’am,” Miranda cadence, radio codes.
- Never wink at the camera; Dale does not know he is Dale in the arena.
- Horror comes from sincerity, not camp — keep politeness intact.
- Cliffhanger partner’s killer can be a long-arc story bait (real or imagined).

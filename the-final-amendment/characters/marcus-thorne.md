## Biography

Marcus Thorne built a frozen-goods transport business from nothing after his father abandoned him. He married Sarah (bakery family), raised Emma (8) and Michael (6), and was known for reliability and quiet kindness.

On **August 16, 1997**, he had promised the kids a beach day. On Interstate 445 a heavily modified vehicle hit their lane. The refrigerated truck’s weight made escape impossible. Marcus watched his family die in twisted metal before his own life nearly ended.

Hospital weeks later, Marcus Thorne was gone. Doctors noted total emotional detachment and fixation on revenge. He took the name **Grave** — already dead, nothing left to lose. He tracked the I-445 massacre to **Glitch** and Glitch’s path into Silus Vane’s games. Grave committed escalating crimes until he was imprisoned too, knowing the arenas were the only way to get close to his family’s killer.

Creepsicle is not just a vehicle — it is his tomb, the metal coffin that carries his family’s ghosts. The flamethrower is the hellfire that took his life; the armor is the wall he built around what remains.

## Character bio audio (transcript)

Canonical VO for the Grave / Marcus Thorne character dossier. Third-person narrator introducing the driver.

> This one... this one's a tragedy. Not a ghost story like the last one. This is a eulogy. The man in this file, Marcus Thorne, is already dead. Been dead for years. What's left is just the tombstone that drives a truck.
>
> Before the fall, Thorne had it all. Ran his own business—Thorne Frozen Transport. Thirty rigs, all of 'em. He was a self-made man. Built it from the ground up after his old man skipped out on him. Met his wife, Sarah, on a delivery run. Two kids, a boy and a girl. From the outside, looking in, he was the American Dream. The file is full of commendations, letters from happy customers. He was a good man. A decent man.
>
> Then came August 16, 1997. Interstate 445.
>
> He was taking his family to the beach. A promise he'd made to his kids for good grades. They were just another family on vacation in a big, refrigerated truck. Then something came out of nowhere. The report says it was a tow truck, driven by a man who just... snapped. We both know who that was. Thorne, being the professional he was, tried to save 'em. Swung the rig, but thirty tons of truck and cargo don't turn on a dime. He watched them die. Then the truck flipped, and he died with them.
>
> Well, almost.
>
> The man who woke up in the hospital three weeks later wasn't Marcus Thorne. The doctors' notes are all the same: complete emotional detachment. Flat affect. No response to stimuli. The man who loved his wife, who played catch with his son, who read bedtime stories to his daughter... he was gone. In his place was this... empty thing. This shell. It was one of the orderlies who first called him 'Grave,' said he looked like a man who'd already been buried. The name stuck.
>
> He didn't talk about revenge. He didn't have to. It was in his eyes. A cold fire. He spent months in that hospital, just asking questions. Who did it. Where was he. When he learned that the animal who killed his family was in Vane's new games... well, that was all she wrote.
>
> He went on a mission. Started small. Bar fights. Assaults. escalated to armed robbery. He was a man with one purpose: get into the system. Get into The Final Amendment. He committed every crime he could think of until they finally gave him what he wanted: a one-way ticket to the arena.
>
> That truck of his... 'The Mourning'... it's a rolling tomb. It's the same model of Thorson rig he was driving that day, but he's had it modified into a weapon. The heavy armor isn't for defense; it's so he can feel nothing when he hits. That flamethrower on top... that's the hell he lives in every day. He's not just trying to win the games. He's trying to burn the memory of that day out of the world, one opponent at a time.
>
> Every person he kills, every car he melts down... it's just practice. Just a warm-up for the main event. For the day he gets his hands on Glitch. He doesn't care about Vane's 'wish.' He doesn't care about getting out. The only thing Marcus Thorne, or what's left of him, wants is to stand over the burning wreckage of that crane truck and know that the thing that killed his family is finally, truly gone.
>
> He's the most dangerous man in the games, not because he's the best driver or has the best weapons. He's dangerous because he's already dead. He's got nothing left to lose. And a man with nothing to lose is a man who can't be beaten.

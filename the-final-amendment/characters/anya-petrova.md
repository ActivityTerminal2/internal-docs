## Biography

Anya Petrova was born into the resistance. Her parents were among the first to oppose **Silus Vane**’s rise; they were captured and executed. She learned to drive almost before she could walk — underground tunnels, back roads, cell-to-cell runs. By sixteen she was the movement’s best courier. Government ops to grab her kept failing. They called it bad luck. She called it skill and intuition. The street called her **Jinx**.

**Wesley Weet** became her mentor and the closest thing to family after her parents. He taught evasion and survival. What Anya did not know: Wesley was feeding the government information in exchange for his own family’s safety.

On a mission to move resistance leaders, Wesley sold her out. The ambush ended a high-speed chase when he remotely killed her ignition. In prison she learned the truth from another captured member. Guards worked her with the punchline that her jinx had run out — that Wesley had finally outsmarted her.

It did not break her. It hardened her. She no longer fights for the resistance’s ideals. She fights for **vengeance** — closing distance on the people who used her, starting with the ghost of Wesley’s betrayal and the regime that bought it.

## Voice / writing notes

- Short sentences; dry humor that cuts, never softens.
- “Jinx” is a name others use; she treats luck as superstition for people who cannot drive.
- Vengeance is personal and specific (Wesley / Vane’s buyers), not abstract revolution slogans — unless a story beat rekindles the cause.
- Avoid making her “edgy teen”; she is forged, not cosplaying toughness.

## Biography

Silas Croft was a brilliant architect who believed in Silus Vane’s unified world government. As a child he was obsessed with Roman engineering and gladiatorial games — spectacle as a valve for human violence. When Vane announced modern arenas, Silas saw a chance to perfect what Rome started.

His early designs were praised: death traps, weapon placements, brutal elegance. The more he built, the more Vane’s cruelty and arbitrary imprisonments unsettled him. He began hiding flaws in the blueprints — escape passages, structural weak points that might fail in the worst matches — small mercies for prisoners he could not openly defend.

His fall came on an arena with a special viewing box for Vane’s **fourth lieutenant** (betting operations). A rival of that lieutenant framed Silas for match-fixing, planting evidence that he had biased the design for a betting ring.

Confronted, Silas could not explain the *real* flaws without admitting sympathy for the condemned. Vane treated it as betrayal: imprison Silas and force him into the games he built. He enters knowing every trap, every weakness, every angle — and that knowledge is both his edge and his punishment.

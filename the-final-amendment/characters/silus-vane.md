## World context

After the Cuban Missile Crisis, national governments fell. A single world government rose in the vacuum — corrupt at the root, led by **Silus Vane**.

Opposition is crushed, framed, or absorbed. Resistance cells (see **Jinx** / Anya Petrova) exist in tunnels and back roads. Enforcement culture is sold as entertainment. Industry and spectacle blur under brands like **Vane Industries**.

---

## Biography & method

Silus speaks like a preacher who never doubts the sermon. He believes — or performs belief — that his way is best for humanity.

He studied **Roman history** and the **Colosseum**: blood and violence as civic distraction. From that, he built modern arena games — an updated “Roman” circuit where the public bets, the state looks strong, and corruption stays off the front page.

### Who fights

- Prisoners (crime, dissent, or both)
- People who spoke against the government
- Framed innocents funneled in to fill brackets and betting lines
- Rare volunteers (e.g. **Glitch** / Lucis Vance)

### Who profits

- Public betting on matches
- Lieutenants managing odds and viewing boxes (Warden’s fall involved Vane’s **fourth lieutenant** and a betting-frame)
- Media and enforcement propaganda riding the ratings

### The winner’s request

Victory is not freedom by default. The champion earns an audience: **one request** from Silus Vane. That promise fills cells and tickets — and is the lever Silus uses to rewrite lives, buy silence, or stage mercy.

## Voice / writing notes

- Cadence: sermon, parable, “we” and “order,” never “I was wrong.”
- Public lines sound generous; private orders are cold logistics.
- Do not soften him into a misunderstood idealist — the Colosseum lesson is the point.
- Distinguish from **Silas Croft** in dialogue and UI (Silus vs Silas).
